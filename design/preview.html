The royal town, plainly told.

The kingdom

Pupupu was the first ruler. Her successors still hold the title Osemawe.

The dialect

People here speak their own Yoruba, distinct from the towns around them.

The markets

Cocoa, yam, and kola nut made Ondo a trading town. The markets still run the day.

The schools

Adeyemi Federal University of Education draws students from across Nigeria.

Places

Six to start with. Open any row.

01 Palace Osemawe Palace

The working seat of the Osemawe, in the old part of town, close to the central markets.

 02 Market Ondo Central Market

Ughele is the largest market: foodstuffs, cloth, kitchen goods, and carriers who will wheel your bags.

 03 Market Iyalaje Market

A second large market on the Akure road, with several entrances and cars moving between the stalls.

 04 Shopping Mukeke Mall

Groceries, a bakery, a pharmacy, and home goods, in a quieter building opposite NCI.

 05 Leisure Nibanola Resort

A quiet resort on the edge of town, with a pool, boats, and horse rides. Activities are paid separately.

 06 Campus Adeyemi Federal University of Education

The federal college that made Ondo a student town, as well as a market town.

The town

A brief history of Ondo

The first ruler is remembered as a woman named Pupupu.

Ondo, also called Ode Ondo, is an old Yoruba town in south-western Nigeria. It is the headquarters of Ondo West, and one of the best known towns in the state.

Oral tradition traces the kingdom to Ile-Ife. The Osemawe remains the traditional ruler and the keeper of custom. People still orient themselves by the palace.

The town grew on farming and trade. Schools came later, and with them a student population. Festivals and the palace keep the older life in view.
